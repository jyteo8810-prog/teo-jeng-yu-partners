Construction Contracts: Five Areas That Can Become Disputes Later

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Construction contracts are drafted at the start of a project, when relationships are cooperative and timelines seem manageable. Disputes tend to surface later, once delay, cost pressure or defects appear. Payment mechanisms are a frequent source of dispute, particularly where certification, valuation and set-off provisions are unclear or inconsistently applied during the course of the project. Variations and instructions are another common flashpoint, especially where changes are made informally on site without being properly recorded or priced in accordance with the contract. Extension of time and delay provisions require careful attention, since the contractual mechanism for claiming additional time can determine whether a contractor is exposed to liquidated damages. Defects liability and handover provisions, together with the allocation of risk for design responsibility, should be reviewed carefully at the outset rather than left until a dispute has already arisen. Our construction practice advises developers, contractors and consultants on contract drafting, risk allocation and disputes arising during and after a project.
