CIPAA Payment Disputes: What Developers and Contractors Should Consider

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Payment disputes are the single most common source of friction on a Malaysian construction project. The Construction Industry Payment and Adjudication Act 2012 (CIPAA) was introduced specifically to address this problem, giving unpaid parties a statutory right to fast-track adjudication instead of waiting years for arbitration or litigation to run its course.

What CIPAA Actually Changes

Before CIPAA, a contractor or subcontractor who was not paid had little practical recourse short of suspending work or commencing full litigation or arbitration, both of which were slow and expensive relative to the sums often in dispute.

CIPAA gives any party to a construction contract (which includes consultancy contracts) the right to refer a payment dispute to adjudication. An adjudication decision is temporarily binding and enforceable as if it were a court judgment, even though it can later be reviewed in arbitration or litigation on the merits.

Critically, CIPAA also renders "pay when paid" clauses void, and prohibits a party from contracting out of its adjudication rights — provisions that once allowed upstream parties to defer payment obligations indefinitely are no longer effective.

Where Parties Get Caught Out

Adjudication under CIPAA runs on strict, short timelines. A payment response, for example, generally must be served within a fixed number of days of the payment claim, and a failure to respond within time can mean the claim is effectively taken as accepted for the purposes of adjudication.

Many disputes are lost or won not on the underlying merits of the claim, but on procedural missteps: a payment claim that does not meet the statutory requirements, a response filed late, or supporting documents that are not properly particularised.

Because the adjudicator's decision is enforceable quickly, a party facing a claim it disputes needs to assess, at speed, whether to contest jurisdiction, dispute the claim on the merits, or apply to stay enforcement — each of which has a different evidential and procedural threshold.

  • Payment claims and responses served within the statutory time limits, with a clear paper trail
  • Variation instructions properly recorded and valued as the contract requires
  • A documented basis for any set-off or back-charge relied on to withhold payment
  • An early view on jurisdictional issues, such as whether the contract is a "construction contract" within the meaning of the Act

What This Means for Developers and Financiers

For developers, a wave of adjudication claims from the main contractor or its subcontractors can materially affect project cash flow and financing covenants, particularly where claims are contested but still need to be budgeted for pending resolution.

Financial institutions financing a development should factor CIPAA exposure into due diligence on drawdown requests and progress claims, since an adjudication decision against the borrower can crystallise a payment obligation faster than the underlying commercial dispute is actually resolved.

Our construction practice advises developers, main contractors, subcontractors and consultants on payment claims, adjudication proceedings, enforcement and set-aside applications under CIPAA.

If your business is facing, or considering bringing, a CIPAA claim, an early assessment of the procedural and merits position can materially change the outcome.
