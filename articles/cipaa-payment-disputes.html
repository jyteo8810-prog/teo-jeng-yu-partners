CIPAA Payment Disputes: What Developers and Contractors Should Consider

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Payment disputes are among the most common sources of friction on a construction project. The Construction Industry Payment and Adjudication Act 2012 (CIPAA) was introduced to give parties a faster route to resolving payment claims without waiting for lengthy arbitration or litigation. Adjudication under CIPAA is intended to preserve cash flow during the life of a project. A party who is not paid what it considers due may refer the dispute to adjudication, and the resulting decision is binding and enforceable, even though it is not final in the way a court judgment is. Developers and contractors should be careful about how payment claims and responses are documented, since strict timelines apply and a failure to respond within the prescribed period can have serious consequences on the outcome of the adjudication. Because an adjudication decision can be enforced quickly, it is important to assess the strength of a payment claim or defence at an early stage, including the underlying contract terms, variation instructions, and the supporting records. Our construction practice advises developers, main contractors, subcontractors and consultants on payment disputes, adjudication proceedings and related contractual issues.
