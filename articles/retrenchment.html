Retrenchment in Malaysia: What Employers Should Get Right

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Retrenchment is a lawful way to reduce a workforce for genuine business reasons — restructuring, redundancy, or a real downturn in business. It is also one of the most frequently disputed areas of Malaysian employment law, because it is so easily, and so often, used incorrectly to remove a specific employee under the cover of a business justification.

Establishing a Genuine Business Reason

A defensible retrenchment exercise starts with a documented commercial justification — a position that is genuinely redundant, a department being reorganised, or a downturn supported by actual financial data — rather than a decision made first and justified afterwards.

The Industrial Court will look closely at whether the retrenchment was in fact used to remove a particular employee, for example where a role is retrenched and then effectively re-created shortly afterwards, or where the employee retrenched happens to be the only one who recently raised a grievance or claim.

Selection Criteria

Where more than one employee could potentially be retrenched from a pool of similar roles, objective and consistently applied selection criteria are essential — commonly including factors such as length of service (the "last in, first out" principle, where applicable), performance records, and skills or qualifications relevant to the business going forward.

Employers should be able to show not just what criteria were used, but why those criteria were chosen and how they were actually applied to each employee considered — a selection process that cannot be reconstructed and explained later is difficult to defend.

Compliance Mechanics

Statutory notice periods, retrenchment and termination benefits (where applicable under the employment contract or the Employment Act), and any requirement to notify the labour authorities for larger-scale exercises should be identified and built into the implementation plan from the start, not addressed after employees have already been informed.

Where retrenchment forms part of a wider restructuring or business transfer, additional considerations arise around the treatment of affected employees' terms and continuity of service, which should be reviewed alongside the retrenchment exercise itself.

  • Document the commercial justification with supporting data before any employee is identified
  • Apply objective, consistent selection criteria across the affected pool
  • Confirm statutory notice, benefits and any authority notification requirements
  • Keep records capable of showing why the exercise was necessary and how it was run

If a Claim Is Brought

An employee who considers a retrenchment unfair may bring a claim for unfair dismissal, and the burden generally falls on the employer to justify both the underlying reason for the exercise and the fairness of the process followed — reinforcing why documentation at the time, not reconstruction afterward, is what actually protects the employer.

Our employment practice advises employers on planning and implementing retrenchment exercises, and on defending unfair dismissal and related claims once they arise.

A retrenchment exercise planned properly at the outset is both more defensible and, in our experience, considerably less likely to end up in the Industrial Court at all.
