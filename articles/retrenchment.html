Retrenchment in Malaysia: What Employers Should Get Right

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Retrenchment is a lawful way to reduce a workforce for genuine business reasons, but it is also one of the most frequently disputed areas of Malaysian employment law when the process is not handled carefully. A defensible retrenchment exercise usually starts with a clear commercial justification, such as redundancy of a position, reorganisation or a genuine downturn in business, rather than being used to remove a particular employee. Employers should apply objective and consistent selection criteria, such as the "last in, first out" principle where relevant, and be able to show why those criteria were chosen and how they were applied. Statutory notice, termination and retrenchment benefits, and any obligation to notify the authorities for larger exercises, should be identified and complied with before implementation, not worked out afterwards. Employees who consider a retrenchment unfair may bring a claim for unfair dismissal, and the employer will need to justify both the reason for the exercise and the fairness of the process followed. Our employment practice advises employers on planning and implementing retrenchment exercises, as well as defending unfair dismissal and related claims.
