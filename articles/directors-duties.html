Directors' Duties in Malaysia: A Practical Overview for Boards

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Directors of a Malaysian company owe duties both under statute and at common law, and a breach can expose a director to personal liability, regardless of how the company itself is performing. Core duties include acting in good faith in the interests of the company, exercising reasonable care, skill and diligence, avoiding conflicts of interest, and not making improper use of position or information for personal gain. These duties do not pause when a company is under financial pressure. Directors of a company facing insolvency should be particularly attentive to the interests of creditors and the risks of continuing to trade, or incurring further debt, without a reasonable prospect of meeting the company's obligations. Board and shareholder decisions, particularly related-party transactions, should be properly documented, with conflicts disclosed and, where appropriate, independent advice obtained. Minority shareholders who consider that a company's affairs have been conducted in a manner unfairly prejudicial to their interests have statutory and common law remedies available, including derivative and oppression actions. Our corporate practice advises boards, directors and shareholders on governance, directors' duties and disputes arising from an alleged breach of those duties.
