Interim Injunctions in Commercial Disputes: When Urgent Relief Is Available

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Some commercial disputes cannot wait for a full trial. Where a party is about to dissipate assets, breach a restrictive covenant or take an irreversible step, an interim injunction may be the only way to preserve the position until the dispute is resolved. Malaysian courts generally consider whether there is a serious question to be tried, whether damages would be an adequate remedy, and where the balance of convenience lies between granting and refusing the relief. Common applications include Mareva injunctions to freeze assets pending judgment, Anton Piller orders to preserve evidence, and prohibitory injunctions to restrain a breach of contract or misuse of confidential information. Urgent applications typically require the applicant to move quickly, present clear evidence and give an undertaking as to damages, since an injunction wrongly obtained can expose the applicant to liability for losses caused to the other side. Because the threshold and evidential requirements are demanding, an early and realistic assessment of the merits is important before deciding whether to apply for urgent relief. Our practice advises corporations and financial institutions on urgent applications, including injunctions and related interim relief, in the context of wider commercial disputes.
