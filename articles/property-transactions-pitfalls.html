Property Transactions: Common Pitfalls in Sale and Purchase Agreements

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

A property sale and purchase agreement looks routine until a dispute arises. By then, the terms that were glossed over at signing are often the ones that determine who bears the loss. Common problem areas include unclear completion and vacant possession dates, inadequate provisions for defects or encumbrances discovered after signing, and ambiguous allocation of risk for delays caused by financing, government approvals or third parties. For developers, standard-form agreements prescribed by housing regulations leave limited room for negotiation, but disputes still commonly arise over liquidated damages for late delivery, defect liability periods and the handover process. For purchasers and investors, due diligence should extend beyond the agreement itself to the title, any charges or caveats, the vendor's authority to sell, and outstanding statutory or maintenance obligations attached to the property. Financial institutions financing a purchase have a related but distinct set of concerns, particularly around the priority and enforceability of their security once the transaction completes. Our practice advises developers, purchasers, investors and financial institutions on property transactions, financing arrangements and disputes arising from them.
