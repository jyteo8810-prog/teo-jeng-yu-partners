Corporate Rescue in Malaysia: Judicial Management and Schemes of Arrangement

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

When a company faces financial distress, the priority is often to preserve the business as a going concern rather than moving straight to liquidation. Malaysian law provides several corporate rescue mechanisms designed to give a distressed company breathing room while a restructuring is worked out. Judicial management places the company's affairs under a court-appointed judicial manager and imposes a moratorium on most claims and enforcement action, giving management time to negotiate with creditors without the immediate threat of winding-up or asset seizure. A scheme of arrangement, whether standalone or alongside a restraining order, allows a company to put a restructuring proposal to its creditors or shareholders for approval, potentially compromising debts or extending repayment terms. For creditors, including financial institutions, the key questions are usually the strength of existing security, the company's realistic prospects of recovery, and how a proposed scheme or moratorium affects their position relative to other creditors. Directors considering these options should act early. Delay narrows the available options and increases the risk of personal exposure where a company continues trading while insolvent. Our practice advises companies, creditors and other stakeholders on restructuring options, judicial management, schemes of arrangement and related insolvency strategy.
