Shareholder Disputes in Malaysia: When a Business Relationship Breaks Down

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Shareholder disputes often arise gradually, through disagreements over management decisions, dividend policy, or the direction of the company, before escalating into a formal dispute. Malaysian company law provides several avenues for an aggrieved shareholder, including an action for oppression where the affairs of the company are conducted in a manner unfairly prejudicial to a shareholder's interests, and derivative actions brought on behalf of the company. Before commencing proceedings, it is usually worth considering the company's constitution, any shareholders' agreement, and whether the underlying dispute can be resolved through negotiation, restructuring of shareholding, or a buy-out arrangement. Shareholder disputes can affect a company's operations, banking relationships and reputation, so early legal input is often valuable in assessing the available options and their likely commercial consequences. Our corporate practice advises shareholders, directors and companies on governance disputes, minority shareholder remedies and related corporate litigation.
