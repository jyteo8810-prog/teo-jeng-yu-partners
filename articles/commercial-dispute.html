A Commercial Dispute Has Started: What Should a Company Do First?

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

The period immediately after a commercial dispute arises is often the most important. Decisions made, or not made, in the first few weeks can shape the strength of a company's position later. Companies should preserve relevant documents and communications as soon as a dispute is anticipated, since gaps in the record can later be used against them, whether the matter proceeds to negotiation, adjudication or litigation. It is important to identify the contractual and legal position early, including relevant notice periods, limitation issues and any dispute resolution mechanism specified in the underlying contract. Companies should also be careful about informal communications made before legal advice is obtained, since statements made in the heat of the moment can affect the company's position later. Our litigation practice advises companies at every stage of a commercial dispute, from initial assessment through to negotiation, proceedings and enforcement.
