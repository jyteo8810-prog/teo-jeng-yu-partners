Domestic Inquiries in Malaysia: Why Process and Documentation Matter

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

A domestic inquiry is the process by which an employer investigates alleged employee misconduct before deciding on disciplinary action, including dismissal. Malaysian courts and the Industrial Court place significant weight on whether the inquiry was conducted fairly, including whether the employee was given adequate notice of the charges, an opportunity to be heard, and whether the inquiry panel acted impartially. Employers who skip or rush the domestic inquiry process, or who fail to keep proper records, may find it difficult to justify a dismissal later, even where the underlying misconduct was serious. Good practice includes clear misconduct policies, consistent application of disciplinary procedures, and careful documentation of every stage of the process, from the initial complaint to the final decision. Our employment practice advises employers on conducting domestic inquiries, disciplinary processes and defending unfair dismissal claims before the Industrial Court.
