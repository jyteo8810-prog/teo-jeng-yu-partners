Domestic Inquiries in Malaysia: Why Process and Documentation Matter

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

A domestic inquiry is the process by which an employer investigates alleged employee misconduct before deciding on disciplinary action, including dismissal. In Malaysia, the fairness of that process is scrutinised almost as closely as the underlying misconduct itself — and a flawed process can undo an otherwise justified dismissal.

Why the Process Is Scrutinised

Under Malaysian employment law, a dismissal must be for just cause or excuse, and the Industrial Court, when reviewing a dismissal, examines both whether the misconduct actually occurred and whether the employer's process in reaching that conclusion was fair.

This means an employer can have genuine, serious misconduct on its hands and still lose an unfair dismissal claim if the employee was not given adequate notice of the charges, a real opportunity to respond, or if the inquiry panel was not seen to be impartial.

The burden in an unfair dismissal claim generally falls on the employer to justify the dismissal — which makes the quality of the employer's contemporaneous documentation, not just its recollection of events, central to defending the decision later.

Getting the Process Right

A defensible process typically starts well before the inquiry itself: a clear, properly communicated code of conduct, a preliminary investigation to establish whether there is a case to answer, and a decision on whether interim suspension (with or without pay, as the contract and law permit) is warranted.

The inquiry itself should give the employee clear written notice of the specific charges, adequate time to prepare, the right to be represented or assisted, and a genuinely independent panel that has not pre-judged the outcome.

The standard of proof applied is the civil standard — on a balance of probabilities — but the panel's findings, and the reasoning behind them, should be recorded in enough detail to withstand later scrutiny.

  • Written notice of charges served with adequate time to prepare
  • An inquiry panel independent of the complainant and not previously involved in the investigation
  • A proper opportunity for the employee to respond and call witnesses
  • Findings and reasoning recorded in writing, not reconstructed after the fact

What This Means for Employers

For corporations and larger employers, the practical risk is less about any single inquiry and more about consistency — inconsistent application of disciplinary standards across similar cases is one of the most common grounds on which an otherwise sound dismissal is successfully challenged.

Retrenchment exercises and misconduct dismissals are legally distinct, and treating one as a substitute for the other (for example, using a retrenchment exercise to remove an underperforming employee without addressing performance directly) creates its own exposure.

Our employment practice advises employers on designing and running domestic inquiries, disciplinary frameworks, and defending unfair dismissal claims before the Industrial Court.

Building a defensible process before a decision is implemented is almost always more effective, and less costly, than defending a decision after the fact.
