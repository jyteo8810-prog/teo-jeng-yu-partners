Enforcing a Judgment in Malaysia: Options After You Win

Legal Insight · TEO JENG YU & PARTNERS

Obtaining judgment is often only the halfway point in a recovery matter. Whether that judgment translates into actual payment depends on the debtor's assets and the enforcement mechanism chosen. Common enforcement tools include a writ of seizure and sale against movable or immovable property, garnishee proceedings against sums owed to the debtor by a third party, and judgment debtor summons to examine the debtor's means. Where the debtor appears unable to pay, a creditor may consider bankruptcy or winding-up proceedings, which can also apply pressure toward settlement even where the ultimate recovery through those proceedings is uncertain. Before committing to a particular enforcement route, it is worth investigating what assets the debtor actually holds, whether they are encumbered by existing security, and whether other creditors are likely to compete for the same assets. Cross-border recovery raises additional questions, including whether the judgment can be recognised or enforced in the jurisdiction where the debtor's assets are located. Our practice advises financial institutions and corporate creditors on the full range of enforcement options following judgment, and on the practical recovery strategy that fits the debtor's position.
